# Synplant 2 Genopatch: how it turns a sample into a patch

Collected 2026-10-28. Primary sources are Sonic Charge's own pages: the User Guide v2.0.2 (Genopatch section pp. 16-18, change history p. 23), the product page, and forum posts by Magnus Lidström (developer) and Fredrik Lidström (his brother and co-founder, who handles support). The other primary source is Magnus's Q&A with CDM. Third-party reviews are marked as such. The True Cuckoo video interview (July 2023, with chapters "Developing and Training the AI" at 24:18 and "Challenging the AI" at 28:35) could not be transcribed here.

Shorthand: **[DEV]** = developer statement, **[DOC]** = official manual or product page, **[3RD]** = review or user, **[INF]** = my inference.

## What the developer has said about how Genopatch works and was built

### Takeaway
Genopatch is "a combination of different machine-learning algorithms" developed over about 10 years. Its neural nets (the focus of the last ~5 years) are trained only on semi-random sounds rendered by Synplant's own engine. The nets learn the inverse mapping from sound to parameters, and they are pruned small enough to run on a CPU. No paper or architecture has been published.

### Cited Findings
- [DEV] "Genopatch is the most challenging endeavor I have ever undertaken. It's a combination of different machine-learning algorithms that I have tweaked over a period of 10 years. For the last five years, I focused mainly on designing and improving the neural nets involved." — [CDM, The road to Synplant 2 (2023-10-16)](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/)
- [DEV] Training data: "I train them on semi-random sounds generated by the synth itself so that it learns how to go backward from output sound to input parameters. The amount of data that it trains on is *massive*. The training takes months for an optimized small net that can run locally on your CPU, and every second that it trains, it plows through hundreds of settings." — [CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/)
- [DEV] "No, it is trained on an endless corpus of algorithmically created sounds." Also: "I can't claim it is trained on only good sounds." — [Sonic Charge forum, How was Synplant 2's ML trained? (2023-10-17)](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2589-how-was-synplant-2s-ml-trained)
- [DEV] "The neural net is trained to learn how the Synplant engine works so that it can make educated guesses on relevant parameter settings for a given input." — [forum 2589](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2589-how-was-synplant-2s-ml-trained)
- [DEV] Asked about Sound2Synth ([arXiv 2205.03043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03043), a deep net for estimating Dexed FM parameters), Magnus said the approaches "seemed similar when I first skimmed through it, but they differed once I started looking into the details. Besides differences in how we approach the neural net, the core difference is that Genopatch doesn't rely solely on the neural network. Team play occurs between different ML paradigms; they feed off each other's work. This happens in real-time on your CPU." — [forum 2589](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2589-how-was-synplant-2s-ml-trained)
- [DEV, Fredrik] "Legally, we own full rights to all the data that Genopatch was trained on." Also: "the trained neural network is only part of the Genopatch process. There's a lot of computation going on. That's why we need 100% of your CPU." — [forum 2589](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2589-how-was-synplant-2s-ml-trained)
- [DEV] Model size and hardware: "I am using homebrew techniques to prune and minimize the neural nets so they are small in download and execute fast on a CPU without needing a GPU. In fact, the performance bottleneck of Genopatch is not running the neural nets but running the Synplant engine to evaluate all the sounds that it generates." He says GPU inference would not gain enough to justify the work. — [forum, Synplant on GPU? (2025-02-02)](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3102-synplant-on-gpu)
- [DEV, Fredrik] "Synplant is CPU only." — [forum 3102](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3102-synplant-on-gpu)
- [DEV] Timeline: "Everything changed when I realized the potential of using machine learning to figure out parameter settings to match audio samples… I spent a few years creating the Genopatch technology, then I discarded all failed attempts with Synplant 2, rewrote the UI from scratch and it took us around 2 years from that point to finish." — [CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/)
- [DEV] Launch post: the road "has been long and paved with abandoned experiments. When we finally solved the machine learning algorithms that made it possible to match synth parameters to a sample, we knew we had something exceptional." — [forum, Synplant 2 Is Here (2023-10-03)](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2466-synplant-2-is-here?page=all)
- [DOC] The search space is "its 48-gene DNA". The manual says Synplant "can't replicate every sound perfectly due to its synthetic nature and the limitations of its 48-gene DNA." — Synplant User Guide v2.0.2 p. 17 ([PDF](https://soniccharge.com/public/Synplant%20User%20Guide.pdf))
- [DEV, Fredrik] A Genopatch result is only a parameter set. Fredrik posted one as a full patch dump: 48 genome values normalised 0-1, including `a_form`, `a_freq`, `a_noise`, `b_form`, `b_freq`, `fm_amt`, `flt_*`, `env_*`, `lfo_*`, `rvb_*`, `saturate`, `sub_am` and `vol_*`, plus a `pitchAdjust` and control values. "No part of the sample is used in the result." — [forum, Does Genopatch Use The Original Sample At All? (2023-10-06)](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2512-does-genopatch-use-the-original-sample-at-all?page=all)
- [DEV, Fredrik] Effects are part of what gets matched. Asked whether a ride cymbal would be matched with heavy reverb, he replied "Exactly this, yes." — [forum, Genopatch audio (2024-06-27)](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2931-genopatch-audio)
- [DOC] The changelog for 2.0.1 (2023-10-29) says "Slightly improved the neural net in Genopatch" and "Genopatch claims slightly less CPU in the Windows version". 2.0.2 (2024-08-29) has no Genopatch changes, and 2.0.2 is still the current version in 2026 (the installer is dated 2024.08.30). — [User Guide change history](https://soniccharge.com/public/Synplant%20User%20Guide.pdf); [Sonic Charge download page](https://soniccharge.com/download); [forum 3360](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3360-phenotype) (Magnus, June 2026: "the latest version of Synplant (version 2.0.2)")
- [DEV, Fredrik, 2026-05-14] "We are working on Genopatch updates and aim to share some details and, god willing, updates later this year." — [forum, Genopatch development and roadmap](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3342-synplant-2-genopatch-system-development-and-future-roadmap)
- [DEV] Related tech from the same team:
  - Dec 2024: "an experimental version of Genopatch trained to create presets from scratch while mimicking the style and quality of the Synplant factory presets" made 30 unique patches per owner. — [forum, Personalized Presets (2024-12-24)](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3073-personalized-presets-new-merch-happy-holidays)
  - Microtonic's Vintage Tonic kits were "created with machine learning from recordings of 10 legendary hardware drum machines". — [download page](https://soniccharge.com/download)
  - June 2026, PhenoType (text to patch). It came from "a new technology for Synplant: an algorithm capable of describing Synplant patches using words and tags". It has about 210 tags and about 1000 synonyms, and its neural nets were trained "on almost 60000 semi-randomly generated patches". It runs locally in Synplant's JavaScript engine. Search method: patches "are randomly generated… The classifier is then used to estimate how well a randomly generated patch matches your request", and it keeps going until it gives up. — [forum, PhenoType (2026-06-10/11)](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3360-phenotype)

### Inferences
- [INF] The training method is self-supervised inversion on synthetic data. Random genomes are rendered by the engine and the net learns audio to genome. This avoids any licensing questions. It matches what Magnus says ("semi-random sounds generated by the synth itself", "go backward from output sound to input parameters") and Fredrik's ownership claim.
- [INF] "Team play between different ML paradigms" most likely means a neural proposal model coupled with an evolutionary or population search. The strands look like independent lineages, and the product is built around genetics. Nobody at Sonic Charge has said "genetic algorithm" or "CMA-ES" in public, so this is speculation.
- [INF] PhenoType (a classifier scoring random candidates in a generate-and-test loop) shows how Magnus likes to build these tools: a small learned evaluator or proposer inside a cheap generate-and-test loop. The 60k-patch number is PhenoType's training set, not Genopatch's. Some secondary write-ups mix the two up.
- [INF] Synplant's engine is two oscillators (A and B) with FM between them, noise, sub, a morphable dual-SVF filter, envelopes, LFO, reverb and saturation. The patch dump shows this, and it is the basis of third-party "2-oscillator FM" descriptions such as [Polarity](https://polarity.me/posts/polarity-music/2023-10-04-synplant-2-this-is-the-future-of-synths-really/). The filter details are in [CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/).

### Gaps
- Net architecture (CNN, MLP, input features such as a mel spectrogram), parameter count and download size: not disclosed. Magnus says only "small", "pruned", "homebrew".
- Dataset size in number of examples, and the training hardware: only "massive" and "hundreds of settings per second" while training, over "months".
- No paper, patent or talk found. The Cuckoo interview chapters probably say more, but I could not transcribe the video.
- The Genopatch install folder `C:\Program Files\Sonic Charge\Synplant Genopatch\` holds a `.numbstrict` config file. Users mention a "Zaylee…" filename in it, possibly a model name. Contents unknown ([forum 2485](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2485-synplant-2-genopatch-causing-pc-crash?page=all)).

## Is the net used for initial guesses and then refined by a search loop? How many candidates, and how fast?

### Takeaway
Yes. The net makes "educated guesses", and Genopatch then renders candidates with the real engine, listens to the results and refines them, hundreds to thousands of renders per second on all CPU cores. Four strands run in parallel, each producing a series of solutions (circles) that get closer as the strand grows. A strand stops when it reaches the top because its solutions converge. Confidence is kept deliberately low, so early guesses are wild.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] Genopatch works "by making educated guesses about optimal synth settings, listening to the outcomes, and iteratively refining them to match the reference audio better." — User Guide p. 16 ([PDF](https://soniccharge.com/public/Synplant%20User%20Guide.pdf))
- [DOC] "You'll then see four strands sprouting upwards, each generating patches. The higher they grow, the closer their solutions (represented by circles) will be to your source audio… Initially, the generated sounds may vary widely… as the strands grow, they converge toward more accurate solutions, each with unique characteristics. Once a strand reaches the top, it stops growing, as the solutions tend to become increasingly similar." — User Guide p. 16
- [DOC] The input is a segment of up to 2 s, chosen from a WAV or AIFF of any rate (files are truncated at 10 min). "Correct Tuning" either tunes to the detected pitch of the reference or maps the sample to C3. Each strand can be restarted on its own. ALT+restart "using the currently loaded Synplant patch as the search base. The solutions will gradually shift from the source patch towards the reference audio as the strand grows". This means the search can start from a given genome. — User Guide pp. 16-18
- [DOC] Auto Preview plays "each new solution… upon discovery", so results stream in as they are found. The Info button shows "detected pitch, solution testing rate, and additional details about selected solutions." — User Guide p. 18
- [DOC] "The speed at which Genopatch produces new results depends on your processor's capabilities. Although it utilizes all available CPU power, it's designed to minimize system lag." Closing the window pauses the search. Closing the DAW project erases the solutions. — User Guide p. 17
- [DEV, Fredrik] "What Genopatch does is run the sound engine in Synplant 2 at hyper-speed, rendering hundreds or even thousands of sounds per second… This is one of the reasons the sound engine is designed to be extremely CPU efficient." It runs at low thread priority and is not recommended for live use. — [forum, Why is Genopatch using 100% CPU? (2023-12-02)](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2689-why-is-genopatch-using-100-cpu)
- [DEV, Fredrik] Like an expert who "quickly dial[s] in a rough start, then listen[s] to the result, tweak a parameter, listen again, and tweak again. This is what Genopatch does, but at hundreds of times per second." — [forum 2512](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2512-does-genopatch-use-the-original-sample-at-all?page=all)
- [DEV] "there are 'confidence' parameters for these machine learning algorithms that I have chosen to keep relatively low, meaning that it will start with pretty wild guesses. The synth engine of Synplant is designed to produce interesting results when you mutate its parameters." — [CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/)
- [DEV] "This happens in real-time on your CPU, and you can see how it develops when you run Genopatch." — [forum 2589](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2589-how-was-synplant-2s-ml-trained)
- [DEV] Low-priority threading caused freezes on some Windows PCs. Magnus shipped a replacement `.numbstrict` config that "is lowering the priority of Genopatch so much that any other process running could potentially grind it to a halt." Freeze reports continued into 2025. — [forum 2485](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2485-synplant-2-genopatch-causing-pc-crash?page=all)
- [3RD] MusicTech says it "uses a lot of CPU while working, but the process has been refined to make it happen in real time." — [MusicTech review](https://musictech.com/reviews/plug-ins/sonic-charges-synplant-2-review/). Sound On Sound notes heavy CPU use. — [SOS review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/sonic-charge-synplant-2)
- [3RD] A Magnus-made script bulk-exports every candidate a run finds, which shows that a run produces many solutions, not one. — [forum 2509](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2509-are-genopatch-candidates-saved-somewhere-temporarily)

### Inferences
- [INF] The loop is probably: net proposes a genome (seeded with low confidence, so it scatters) → engine renders → similarity measured → a refinement step (likely evolutionary, given four independent lineages that each converge) → better solutions are emitted as circles. Rendering is the cost, not the net, which says the search does many evaluations per net call.
- [INF] Four strands with separate restarts are a diversity mechanism: one population or run per strand, so the user gets four different families of answer instead of one point estimate. This is how the product handles ambiguity (many genomes sound alike).
- [INF] At "hundreds to thousands" of renders per second, each candidate (≤2 s of audio) must be rendered much faster than real time and possibly shorter or at a lower rate. That is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- Exact candidates per strand, the total number of solutions per run, and typical time to a good match are not published. The Info panel shows a "solution testing rate", but no published numbers. Reviews describe it qualitatively (results start within seconds and keep improving).
- Whether candidates are rendered at full length or quality during the search is not stated.

## What similarity measure is used?

### Takeaway
Not disclosed. Sonic Charge says only that it is "listening to the outcomes". There is no public statement on whether the loss is spectral, perceptual or a learned embedding.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] "listening to the outcomes, and iteratively refining them to match the reference audio better." — User Guide p. 16
- [DEV] The matching target includes effects (reverb is used to mimic cymbals). — [forum 2931](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2931-genopatch-audio)
- [DEV] Pitch is detected separately ("detected pitch" in Info, and the Correct Tuning option). — User Guide pp. 17-18
- [3RD] Users report that Genopatch "struggles to find convincing solutions to the timbral complexity of foley, composited sound effects… and polyphonic instrument sounds". — [forum 2680 (user)](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2680-synplant-2-0-1-resynthesizing-complex-sound-effects). A beta tester says it gives "its best shot" on chords, vocals and field recordings. — [forum 2278](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2278-coming-in-2023?page=all)

### Inferences
- [INF] The similarity measure probably compares time-frequency representations of the whole ≤2 s segment, since envelope, filter motion and reverb tail all get matched. Whether this is hand-made features (mel or MFCC) or the net's own learned embedding is unknown. A learned embedding would fit "team play" (the net both proposes and scores), but that is speculation.

### Gaps
- Loss or feature definition, pitch handling inside the loss, and the weighting of envelope against timbre are not published.

## Development time and problems the developer reported

### Takeaway
About 10 years of ML tinkering, the last ~5 on neural nets, a few years focused on Genopatch, then ~2 years to ship after scrapping earlier Synplant 2 attempts. Problems he named: getting the inversion to work at all, keeping the nets small for CPU, making the engine fast enough to be the search's inner loop, picking low confidence settings so results stay interesting, Windows CPU-load and freeze issues, and the limits of a 48-gene synth.

### Cited Findings
- [DEV] Durations and the "Duke Nukem Forever" remark (v1 in 2009, v2 in Oct 2023). — [CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/)
- [DEV] "abandoned experiments… When we finally solved the machine learning algorithms". — [forum 2466](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2466-synplant-2-is-here?page=all)
- [DEV] Nets kept small by home-made pruning. The engine render is the bottleneck. — [forum 3102](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3102-synplant-on-gpu)
- [DEV] The engine was built to be very CPU-efficient so Genopatch can run faster. — [forum 2689](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2689-why-is-genopatch-using-100-cpu)
- [DEV] Low confidence was chosen on purpose: wild early guesses are "fun" because the engine mutates well. — [CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/)
- [DOC] Ambiguity: strands converge to "increasingly similar" solutions, so growth stops at the top. Each strand keeps "unique characteristics". — User Guide p. 16
- [DEV] Narrow sounds versus broad ones (said about PhenoType, a related problem): "An oboe has a fairly narrow definition… finding that relatively small area of the parameter space is a much tougher problem for the networks." — [forum 3360](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3360-phenotype)
- [DEV] Windows freezes, possibly thermal throttling or overclocking, were handled with a lower-priority config. — [forum 2485](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2485-synplant-2-genopatch-causing-pc-crash?page=all)

### Inferences
- [INF] Lessons for Zero Engine's Reference Match:
  1. The fast-engine-as-inner-loop design matches ours. Their render-bound finding fits our light search renders.
  2. A proposal net trained purely on our own engine's renders is the licence-clean route they took.
  3. Several independent strands for diversity, instead of one best answer, may suit an ambiguous inverse problem.
  4. A search seeded from the current patch (their ALT+restart) is a cheap feature.

### Gaps
- No public account of specific failed approaches, of how many-to-one ambiguity is handled in training (for example a multi-modal output head), or of measured accuracy.
