# Synplant guesses with nets, then renders to decide

Synplant 2's Genopatch turns a sample into a patch in two steps. First, small neural nets trained **only on semi-random sounds rendered by Synplant's own engine** make "educated guesses" at the parameters. Then a CPU search **renders hundreds to thousands of candidates per second**, listens to them and refines, across four parallel "strands" that each settle on a different answer ([CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/); [Sonic Charge forum 2689](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2689-why-is-genopatch-using-100-cpu)). The developer says the net is not the bottleneck. Rendering the engine is, and the engine was built to be fast because of that ([forum 3102](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3102-synplant-on-gpu)). Sonic Charge's later tools reuse this pattern of a small learned model inside a render-or-generate-and-test loop. An experimental Genopatch made presets from scratch "while mimicking the style and quality" of the factory presets ([forum 3073](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3073-personalized-presets-new-merch-happy-holidays)). PhenoType (June 2026) generates random patches and keeps the ones a tag classifier likes ([forum 3360](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3360-phenotype)). The results hold up on short, simple, synth-made one-shots and drift on layered, modulated, acoustic or polyphonic sources. Most reviewers treat it as a variation engine, not a cloner. Zero Engine's Reference Match already goes further on input handling (note transcription, stereo, loops) and already has the render-and-check search. What it lacks is the learned first guess. For Generate, the useful lesson is the PhenoType pattern: a small net trained on the engine's own renders that scores or proposes in-genre candidates, with the existing render check still deciding.

## A 48-gene engine built to be inverted

Genopatch searches a deliberately small space. The manual calls it Synplant's "48-gene DNA" and blames that for the sounds it cannot reach ([User Guide 2.0.2](https://soniccharge.com/public/Synplant%20User%20Guide.pdf)). A result is nothing but those genes. Co-founder Fredrik Lidström posted one as 48 values normalised 0–1 (`a_form`, `a_freq`, `fm_amt`, `flt_*`, `env_*`, `lfo_*`, `rvb_*`, `saturate`, …) and stressed that "no part of the sample is used in the result" ([forum 2512](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2512-does-genopatch-use-the-original-sample-at-all?page=all)). The engine has **two oscillators with FM, a morphable dual-SVF filter, envelopes and an LFO, per-voice asymmetric saturation, a reverb with its own attack envelope, a two-band EQ and a soft clipper** ([CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/); [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/sonic-charge-synplant-2)). Effects are matched too: asked whether a ride cymbal would come back as a tone drowned in reverb, Fredrik answered "Exactly this, yes" ([forum 2931](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2931-genopatch-audio)). Magnus Lidström says the engine "is designed to produce interesting results when you mutate its parameters" ([CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/)). That matters as much as accuracy, because random and mutated settings stay musical. Zero Engine's parameter space is much larger (wavetables, unison, OTT, a spectral stage, macros). That makes inversion harder and makes any learned prior worth more, because blind search wastes renders in a big space.

The input side is plain. Genopatch takes **up to about 2 s** of a WAV or AIFF, chosen with a position slider, **sums it to mono**, detects pitch and tunes the result to it (a "Correct Tuning" option can fail on complex sounds), and treats the window as one sound event ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/reviews/plug-ins/sonic-charges-synplant-2-review/); [User Guide](https://soniccharge.com/public/Synplant%20User%20Guide.pdf)). It does not transcribe notes. A chord sample gives a chord on every key, and support's advice is to isolate one note first ([forum 2961](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2961-from-audio-sample-of-a-chord-to-individual-notes)). ALT+restart seeds a strand from the loaded patch, so it "gradually shift[s] from the source patch towards the reference" ([User Guide](https://soniccharge.com/public/Synplant%20User%20Guide.pdf)). Zero Engine's Reference Match is already ahead of all of this: it transcribes notes and chords, replays them with a searched gate, measures width, and handles a loop that opens quietly.

## Nets propose, the engine judges, four strands keep variety

Magnus describes Genopatch as "a combination of different machine-learning algorithms" tuned over ten years, the last five mainly on the nets. They are trained "on semi-random sounds generated by the synth itself so that it learns how to go backward from output sound to input parameters", on a "massive" amount of data for months, "plow[ing] through hundreds of settings" every second ([CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/)). He admits the corpus is algorithmic and "I can't claim it is trained on only good sounds", and Fredrik adds that "we own full rights to all the data that Genopatch was trained on" ([forum 2589](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2589-how-was-synplant-2s-ml-trained)). The key difference from academic nets such as Sound2Synth is that "Genopatch doesn't rely solely on the neural network. Team play occurs between different ML paradigms" in real time on the CPU ([forum 2589](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2589-how-was-synplant-2s-ml-trained)). The nets are pruned with "homebrew techniques" so they are small and need no GPU ([forum 3102](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3102-synplant-on-gpu)).

The search is visible. Four strands grow, each emitting candidates ("circles") that get closer as it climbs, and a strand stops at the top once its solutions stop changing ([User Guide](https://soniccharge.com/public/Synplant%20User%20Guide.pdf)). Magnus keeps the nets' **"confidence" parameters deliberately low, so it "will start with pretty wild guesses"** ([CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/)). That is a design choice for variety and fun, not a defect. Results stream in (Auto Preview plays each one as it is found), the search runs at low thread priority, and on some Windows machines it pins the CPU and has frozen PCs ([forum 2485](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2485-synplant-2-genopatch-causing-pc-crash?page=all)). Several things are **undisclosed**: the net architecture, its input features, the similarity measure (only "listening to the outcomes"), whether search renders are shortened, and the refinement algorithm. Reading "team play" as a net proposer plus an evolutionary population search is plausible but unconfirmed. Genopatch's only update since launch was "slightly improved the neural net" in 2.0.1. 2.0.2 (2024) is still current, and in May 2026 Sonic Charge said only that updates are being worked on ([forum 3342](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3342-synplant-2-genopatch-system-development-and-future-roadmap)).

| | Synplant Genopatch | Zero Engine Reference Match |
|---|---|---|
| First guess | Small nets trained on own renders | Rank all 72 presets at the reference's pitch (MatchNet in progress) |
| Refinement | Undisclosed "team play", hundreds to thousands of renders/s | Pattern search over 23 settings, 6 workers, second round for 2 |
| Diversity | 4 strands, each a different family | Finalists collapse to one result |
| Input | ≤2 s, mono, one event | Whole phrase, stereo, notes transcribed and replayed |
| Loss | Undisclosed | Mel + MFCC + centroid + loudness + width |
| Seed from current patch | Yes (ALT+restart) | No |

## Simple synth one-shots match, layered and modulated sources drift

Reviewers agree on where it works. MusicRadar finds synth-made sources often come "close to bang-on", with less accuracy on non-synthetic ones ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/sonic-charge-synplant-2-review)). Sound On Sound says it is **best on short plucks, basses and percussion**, and that a playable guitar instrument from a 2 s note is an unrealistic expectation ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/sonic-charge-synplant-2)). MusicTech advises treating results as variations and lists mono analysis as a con ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/reviews/plug-ins/sonic-charges-synplant-2-review/)). Users split sharply on bass and drums. On Dogs on Acid one producer calls it incredible on bass, kicks and hats, another calls it very poor, and a tester reports problems with "even slightly complex sounds, let alone neuro basses" ([Dogs on Acid](https://www.dogsonacid.com/threads/synplant-2-generate-patches-from-any-sample.822242/page-2)). The split makes sense if simple 808s and subtractive basses land and layered, resampled bass-music sounds do not. The failure reports recur: rhythmic complexity ([KVR](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=595090&start=315)), foley and polyphonic sources ([forum 2680](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2680-synplant-2-0-1-resynthesizing-complex-sound-effects)), **dry one-shots coming back with reverb and looping tails** ([forum 2583](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/2583-feature-request-some-manual-guidance-for-genopatch)), and patches that are not playable across the keyboard without tweaking. Users still prize the four-way choice. Testers of MicroMusic, the Vital competitor, complain it returns one static preset with no envelopes or modulation ([Dogs on Acid](https://www.dogsonacid.com/threads/micro-music-ai-synth-replicator-for-vital.823849/)). No review or user gives numbers: no hit rates, no similarity scores, no time to a good match beyond "a few minutes" for long soundscapes.

The reverb complaint is the most useful for Zero Engine. When a loss rewards matching a decay, effects that fill in the decay make an easy shortcut. Reference Match already re-scores finalists with reverb/delay and the spectral stage off, and that habit should stay. The keyboard complaint is a second lesson: a match fitted at one pitch is never checked elsewhere. The academic sound-matching work tests on in-domain synthetic targets ([arXiv 2407.16643](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16643)), and recent work names the off-manifold problem, references the synth cannot make, as the real gap ([arXiv 2609.29320](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29320)). Matchbench's engine-made targets overstate real-world accuracy in the same way.

## Lessons for Reference Match: a learned start, kept honest by renders

The Genopatch design confirms Zero Engine's choices: a fast engine as the inner loop, a search that renders rather than trusting a model, and light search renders with full-quality finalists. The untracked `Source/MatchNet.*` already follows Sonic Charge's route: an MLP on the first note's log-mel at four time slices, trained on engine renders, that proposes presets, tables and settings as extra search starts. What Genopatch adds is concrete.

**Make the net a proposer, never the answer.** Feed its top guesses into the same pattern search alongside the best-ranked presets, and judge only by rendered loss. That matches "team play" and keeps a bad net from making Match worse. Accept a guess only when matchbench and the owner's references improve with the net on versus off.

**Keep the guesses wide.** Magnus's low confidence argues for taking several of the net's top presets and sampling settings around its output, not one argmax, because the inverse problem is many-to-one.

**Return several finalists.** Expose two to four distinct finalists (different presets or wavetables), the way Genopatch's four strands do, instead of collapsing to one. Variety is the feature users praise most, and it hides near-misses.

**Seed from the current patch.** Add a "match from here" mode that starts the search at the loaded settings. Genopatch's ALT+restart shows it is cheap and useful.

**Speed buys more than cleverness.** Sonic Charge says rendering, not the net, is the cost. Speed-ups to light renders (shorter windows, fewer voices, a lower rate during the search) raise match quality directly within the ~20 s budget.

**Guard against FX shortcuts and single-pitch fits.** Keep the FX-off re-score, add a dry-reference penalty when the reference's tail is short, and render one octave up and down on the finalist as a playability check.

## Lessons for Generate: a small judge or proposer trained on the engine

Generate already uses render-and-check: `generateProfile` makes in-genre random variations, and Generate renders 6 candidates and keeps one that passes. The two later Sonic Charge projects fit this directly. The experimental "presets from scratch" Genopatch learned the **style and quality of the factory presets** ([forum 3073](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3073-personalized-presets-new-merch-happy-holidays)). PhenoType trained a tag classifier "on almost 60000 semi-randomly generated patches" and then **generates random patches and asks the classifier how well each matches the request**, running locally in Synplant's script engine ([forum 3360](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3360-phenotype)). Magnus also notes narrow targets are hard: an oboe occupies "a relatively small area of the parameter space", so it is tougher to find ([forum 3360](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3360-phenotype)). Reviewers find PhenoType's sounds often basic but usable ([Polarity](https://polarity.me/posts/polarity-music/2026-06-10-synplant-2-phenotype-review-ai-text-to-patch-synth/)).

Two designs follow, and the cheap one comes first. The first is a **critic net**: a small MLP reading the same SoundPrint features MatchNet uses, trained to say "which genre/type does this render sound like, and is it a keeper". Generate draws far more than 6 random candidates from `generateProfile`, renders them light, scores them with the critic, and runs the existing full check only on the best few. This changes nothing in the DSP, and the critic only reorders candidates the current generator would already make, so it cannot produce something worse than today. The second is a **proposer net** that outputs settings directly (a conditional VAE or a model that predicts the next setting given the others, conditioned on genre/type). It can move beyond `generateProfile`'s hand-written ranges, which is where real novelty comes from. It also needs more data, risks bland averaged patches, and still needs the render check. Sonic Charge itself kept random generation plus a learned judge for PhenoType, which is a good sign the first design is enough.

Training data stays clean if it comes from the same places Sonic Charge used. **Positives** are the 72 designed profiles, the owner's favourites in `%APPDATA%/Zero Engine/Favourites`, and Generate results the owner keeps. **Negatives** are Generate results that fail the check or that the owner skips. **Genre labels** come from renders of the engine itself, which can be produced without limit. The owner's own recordings (the Jupiter-8 sessions) can join as positives for tone. Commercial packs (Splice, Loopmasters, NI) must stay out, even as unlabelled "what good sounds like" examples, because their licences forbid AI training even for personal use. Freesound CC0 is licence-clean but proved too mixed to use wholesale. Logging keep/skip per Generate click costs little and turns the owner's taste into the training signal. That is the closest equivalent to Sonic Charge's "quality of the factory presets".

## Conclusion

Genopatch is not a model that knows the answer. It is a fast synth wrapped in a search, with a small learned prior that saves renders and adds variety. Zero Engine already has the harder parts: a stronger input model (notes, chords, stereo, loops) and a render-judged search. The gains on offer are the learned first guess, several returned answers, and seeding from the current patch. For Generate, the evidence points away from a large generative model and toward a small critic trained on the engine's own renders and the owner's keep/skip choices, ranking many cheap random candidates before the existing render check. It is licence-clean, real-time safe because it runs off the audio thread, and fits the simple-but-powerful brief. One caution: Sonic Charge has published no accuracy numbers in three years, so any claim here that a net "helps" has to be proved on matchbench and the owner's references, not assumed.
