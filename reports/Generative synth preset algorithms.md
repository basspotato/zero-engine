# Render, judge, archive: how Generate should grow

The methods that reliably produce big, interesting synth patches share one shape: **sample near known-good presets, render every candidate, judge it with several capped measures, and keep a diverse pool instead of one champion**. Large generative models are not what makes them work. Pure randomizers fail in documented ways (silence, bad pitch, "nonsense variations"). Academic ML "preset generators" are almost all audio-to-parameter inversion or interpolation models, and the one shipping product closest to Zero Engine's Generate, Sonic Charge's PhenoType, generates random patches and lets a small classifier rank them. Zero Engine's Generate already has the render-and-check core most of the field converges on. Its gaps sit around that core. The scores reward broadband width, raw spectral movement and loudness-adjacent traits, which the literature says get gamed. Diversity comes only from a six-result novelty memory, not an archive. Each candidate is heard at one pitch. Nothing records which results the owner keeps. The highest-value work is therefore cheap and non-ML: harden the score, log keeps and skips, widen the candidate pool with a short pre-screen render, and add a small quality-diversity archive. A Bradley-Terry taste model on SoundTraits comes next. It needs only tens of judgments and microseconds of CPU. The planned neural judge pays off after that, trained on engine renders as PhenoType's classifier was. A proposer network that writes settings directly comes last, and only if the judge and archive plateau.

## Unconstrained randomness fails; sampling near good presets works

Four decades of evolutionary sound design split into two jobs: **target matching**, where fitness is distance to a reference, and **interactive evolution**, where the listener is the fitness function. The operator settings differ by job. In Johnson's FM work, matching worked best with 0.1 % mutation and 60 % crossover, levelling off after about 100 generations. Searching for *new* sounds worked with crossover near 100 % and mutation as high as 10 % ([Johnson 1995](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~djohnson/Files/thesis1995.pdf)). So exploration wants roughly 10 to 100 times more mutation than matching. Generate's push step, which moves three searched settings by up to ±0.15 in unit range, sits at the matching end. That suits "polish the pick" and does little for "find something new".

The commercial and community record is blunt about naive randomness. The open-source u-he preset randomizer offers three modes: sample each parameter from **the value distributions in your own preset library**, randomize an existing preset by an amount, or merge several presets. Even so, an early user reported patches that went silent, odd pitch shifts and "nonsense variations" and asked for stability locks. A "stable mode" that freezes pitch-like parameters is still only a listed idea ([README](https://unpkg.com/u-he-preset-randomizer@1.2.0/README.md); [KVR](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=8898478)). Dexed's only "randomizer" is a sysex loader that clamps every parameter into range ([Dexed issue 389](https://www.github.com/asb2m10/dexed/issues/389)). The better-received tools all constrain randomness with knowledge of good sounds. neuralDX7 squeezes the DX7's 155 parameters into an 8-dimensional VAE latent and samples there, so random draws come out plausible ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/ai-generated-fm-patches-free)). Synplant grows branches outward from a seed, so small steps keep most variants usable ([CDM](https://cdm.link/synplant-2-hands-on/)). Magnus Lidström says Synplant's engine "is designed to produce interesting results when you mutate its parameters" ([CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/)).

Zero Engine already follows the winning pattern. `generateProfile` varies around 72 designed profiles, and every candidate is rendered and checked before anyone hears it. The Free mode's two-genre blends (searched settings mixed 25 to 75 %, structural choices such as the wavetable taken whole from one parent) match the merge mode above and the standard practice of interpolating continuous settings only. Research blending uses learned latents because raw per-parameter interpolation sounds uneven. A transformer autoencoder gave paths **12.6 % smoother on average than linear interpolation** on Dexed ([arXiv 2210.16984](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.16984)). Rendering and checking each blend, as Generate does, is the cheap substitute for that latent, and it is enough at this scale.

## Quality-diversity search keeps "interesting" alive across clicks

Quality-diversity (QD) search is the main answer in the literature to "big *and* interesting". MAP-Elites keeps **one elite per cell of a behaviour grid**, so the pool stays diverse whatever the fitness says ([Mouret & Clune, arXiv 1504.04909](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.04909)). For synths specifically, Masuda and Saito added a novelty objective to genetic sound matching on Dexed and u-he Diva, with spectral centroid and flatness as the behaviour descriptors. Novelty kept the population diverse, which **improved match quality**, and the QD variants returned several distinct good matches ([J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ipsjjip/31/0/31_220/_article/-char/en); [DAFx paper](https://dafx2020.mdw.ac.at/proceedings/papers/DAFx20in21_paper_46.pdf)). The largest body of work is Jónsson and colleagues' kromosynth, which evolves CPPN-plus-DSP-graph genomes with MAP-Elites. Two findings transfer. Behaviour spaces learned without labels (PCA or autoencoders over audio features, **retrained on sounds found during the search**) gave significantly more diversity than hand-made two-descriptor grids, and quality was judged separately from diversity ([arXiv 2512.02783](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.02783)). The other finding is "goal switching": a candidate that is mediocre in its own niche becomes the stepping stone to an elite in another ([arXiv 2606.09780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09780)). The budgets do not transfer. Those runs use **300,000 evaluations** offline, far beyond one click.

Applied to Generate, the lesson is to keep an archive rather than rerun a fresh search on every click. Today each click draws 24 new variations, and novelty is measured only against the last six results, so two sessions apart Generate can hand back the same "best" corner of a type. A small per-genre/type archive changes that. It could be keyed on two or three SoundTraits (brightness × movement × width, say), filled by every click and by an optional background or offline `ZeroAnalyze` fill, and saved next to favourites. Then Generate could sample from occupied cells it has not shown lately and mutate from them. Mutating an archived elite gives the stepping-stone effect almost for free. No published work applies MAP-Elites to a conventional parametric synth's presets for generation rather than matching, so this step is a reasoned transfer, not a proven recipe.

## Every measure of "big" gets gamed unless capped, gated and loudness-matched

Perceptual research gives Generate's traits a firmer footing, and it also warns where they break. Timbre adjectives cluster into three dimensions: **luminance** (bright), **texture** (rough) and **mass** (dense, rich, full, thick) ([Zacharakis et al. 2014](https://joshreiss.github.io/documents/2014/Zacharakis%20Pastiadis%20Reiss%20-%20Music%20Perception.pdf)). Luminance and texture match measurable perceptual dimensions well. **Mass showed no strong acoustic correlate** ([Zacharakis et al. 2015](https://joshreiss.github.io/documents/2015/Zacharakis%20Pastiadis%20Reiss%20-%20Music%20Perception%202015.pdf)). On FM synths, participants chased thickness through amplitude envelopes, not spectrum ([Hayes et al.](https://comma.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/assets/pdf/hayes_perceptual_2021.pdf)). "Body" and "richness" are therefore Generate's least reliable traits, and the owner's own sounds are the right arbiter for them. Other targets are measurable. A punch model based on 95th-percentile weighted transient loudness (PM95) reached **Pearson r = 0.849** with listeners ([JAES 2019](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/en/publications/a-perceptual-model-of-punch-based-on-weighted-transient-loudness/)), and a feature regression predicted heaviness/hardness at **r = 0.86**, driven by sensory dissonance, loudness, treble and spectral flux ([Czedik-Eysenberg et al. 2017](https://dl.gi.de/items/5581898c-a10d-4603-9d4e-9138eaecc459/full)). Perceived width depends on frequency: lower-frequency sounds seem wider at the same coherence ([Käsbach et al.](https://core.ac.uk/download/20608606.pdf)), and on headphones decorrelation broadens width most around **400 to 800 Hz** ([UTS 2020](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/137853)).

Each of these correlates is also a trap. Heaviness rises with loudness, treble and dissonance, so "maximise big" drifts towards harsh. Optimising one reward model inflates that metric far more than others, a failure called "verifier hacking". In one test, CLAP-only training raised CLAP by 10.8 % and production quality by 1.4 %, and standardised composites of several judges reduced the effect ([SCORE, arXiv 2509.19831](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.19831)). Fine-tuning a piano model on Audiobox Aesthetics improved its ratings but **sharply reduced output diversity** ([SMART, arXiv 2504.16839](https://awesomepapers.io/speech-audio/papers/2504.16839)). Distribution-matching rewards built from exemplar sets did better than single scores ([DRAGON, arXiv 2504.15217](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.15217v2)). That supports scoring against the owner's 1,187 sounds rather than chasing extremes.

Generate's current score already does much of this: z-scores in the owner's per-type spreads, a cap at +1.5, an in-character gate and loudness held near the designed profile. Three weak points remain. **Width** is broadband side share, so a sound can score as wide by decorrelating its low end or its top octave. The fix is to score side share in roughly 200 Hz to 2 kHz with a floor on low-band correlation. **Movement** is mean spectral change, which noise and random jitter inflate. It should be measured on harmonic bins, or rewarded only when periodic, and gated by the harmonic-to-noise floor that `ZeroAnalyze noise` already computes. **Nothing penalises loud-bright-rough together**. The Zwicker-Fastl annoyance form, loudness × (1 + √(sharpness² + roughness/fluctuation terms)), is a ready-made penalty shape ([metricgate](https://metricgate.com/docs/psychoacoustic-annoyance/)). The cap also deserves a change from "flat above +1.5 SD" to a tent that falls beyond it, so overshoot is discouraged rather than merely unrewarded.

Learned judges belong offline. Audiobox Aesthetics scores four axes, of which Production Quality and Content Usefulness best match "release-ready". It is CC-BY 4.0 but runs as a WavLM-sized PyTorch transformer ([arXiv 2502.05139](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05139); [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/audiobox-aesthetics)), and nobody has validated it on single synth notes. FAD and KAD measure whether a *set* of renders sits in the same distribution as a reference set. That suits per-type evaluation of Generate's output against the owner's sounds, and KAD converges with fewer samples ([arXiv 2502.15602](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15602v2)). FAD's agreement with listeners depends heavily on the embedding: one embedding gave Spearman above 0.5 against ratings and VGGish below 0.1 ([arXiv 2403.17508](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17508)). Measuring commercial pack audio this way is fine under the ground rules, because it is measurement, not training.

## ML generators mostly invert sounds; the shipping pattern is generate-then-judge

The ML literature is less useful for "make a new good patch" than its volume suggests. FlowSynth (VAE plus normalizing flows, about 11k Diva sounds) learns latent macro controls and supports exploration ([arXiv 1907.00971](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00971v1); [review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.02490)). Le Vaillant's VAEs train on more than 30k Dexed presets with mixed continuous and categorical heads ([preset-gen-vae](https://gwendal-lv.github.io/preset-gen-vae)). The 2025-2026 models (equivariant flow matching on Surge XT, masked discrete diffusion with render-reward GRPO on Dexed) are all **inversion** models ([arXiv 2506.07199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07199v1); [DDSynth-RL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03032)). The search found no paper doing unconditional or tag-conditioned diffusion of new presets. Their one transferable lesson concerns blandness. Regression averages across the many settings that give the same sound, and modelling a distribution instead "substantially improves performance" ([Hayes et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07199v1)). Any proposer Zero Engine trains must be *sampled* (a VAE at temperature above 1, discrete diffusion or a flow), never a regressor.

The data story favours Zero Engine. Own-engine renders are the standard training source. Uniformly random parameters land far from musical presets, though, and proxies trained on 10M random presets **transfer poorly to hand-made presets** ([arXiv 2509.07635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07635v1); [summary](https://pith.science/paper/2509.07635)). The NI Massive work sampled from the factory-preset distribution for that reason ([arXiv 2407.16643](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16643)). Generate's own candidates are already that kind of preset-like distribution, so tens of thousands of labelled renders (genre, type, passed or failed the check, traits) cost hours offline. That matches the 30k to 60k scale of Dexed and PhenoType.

Products confirm the generate-then-judge design. PhenoType's classifier was trained on almost 60,000 semi-randomly generated patches. It **generates random patches and asks the classifier how well each matches the request**, shows a confidence score, and runs locally ([Sonic Charge forum 3360](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3360-phenotype); [CDM](https://cdm.link/synplant-phenotype/)). CDM calls it "a randomizer with intention". Genopatch's nets are trained only on Synplant's own renders, and its developer says rendering, not the net, is the bottleneck ([CDM](https://cdm.link/the-road-to-synplant-2/); [forum 3102](https://soniccharge.com/forum/topic/3102-synplant-on-gpu)). CTAG shows a third route with no trained generator at all: gradient-free search over a 78-parameter synth scored by a frozen CLAP embedding ([arXiv 2406.00294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00294)). All three put a fast engine inside the loop and keep the model small. Inference cost is a non-issue. Generate already runs on worker threads, ONNX Runtime is fastest for stateless models, and every inference engine overruns deadlines when it runs on the audio callback ([ANIRA, arXiv 2506.12665](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12665)).

## Tens of judgments suffice to learn one owner's taste if the model stays small

Learning one person's taste from 20 to 200 judgments is a solved-enough problem, provided the features are fixed and informative and the model has few parameters. The base model is a latent utility with a pairwise likelihood. Chu and Ghahramani's GP preference model scales linearly with the number of judgments ([ICML 2005](https://mlg.eng.cam.ac.uk/zoubin/papers/icml05chuwei-pl.pdf)). For audio, a GP over a 5-band EQ found settings **significantly preferred over random experimentation with 12 listeners** ([AES 2013](https://www.aes.org/e-lib/browse.cfm?elib=17048)). GP active learning learned perceptual synth knobs faster than baselines, though only in simulation ([Huang et al. IUI 2014](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/huang14knobs.pdf)). SocialEQ needed **about 25 ratings per user** before transfer from prior users cut the number ([Kim & Pardo 2014](https://interactiveaudiolab.github.io/assets/papers/Kim_Pardo_ICMLA2014.pdf)). Google's PIPE models a user as a linear mix of base "types", fitted by logistic regression on pairs ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/pipe-personalized-image-generation-via-preference-encoding/)). In Zero Engine terms, the per-type targets from the owner's sounds are the prior, and the owner's picks shift a few weights. All of these studies are small (6 to 12 people), and **none tests synth presets with preferential optimisation**, so expect gains of that kind, not of a proven size.

Signal quality matters more than model choice. Clicks are biased as absolute labels but reasonably accurate as **relative** preferences ([Joachims et al. SIGIR 2005](https://www.sigir.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/p003.pdf)). Pairwise judgments carry less measurement error than ratings ([Webb & Picinali 2025](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/fa2025/data/articles/000660.pdf)). Deezer weights each interaction's reliability by how often it repeats ([Deezer](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2025/06/umap-sguerra-html/)). For Generate, that gives a ranked ladder: a favourite saved, then a result kept until the session is saved, then a return to a history item, with a quick re-generate as only a weak negative. Each should be logged as "X beat the candidates shown with it". The search found no peer-reviewed validation of regenerate speed as a dislike signal. Interactive evolution adds a usability lesson. Fatigue is its main limit, and showing a subset while asking only occasionally cut user evaluations **tenfold** ([Quiroz et al. 2009](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~quiroz/inc/docs/trans2009.pdf)). The rating predictors that worked best weighted recent data ([Wang, Wang, Takagi](https://catalog.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/opac_download_md/1808896/WSTST2005.pdf)). Generate's one-gesture click already respects the fatigue limit, so the taste model should learn silently from the log and never ask for ratings.

Collapse onto one style is the risk. The remedies are a learned term kept soft and capped (as the owner-sound guide already is), Thompson sampling from the model's posterior so exploration is built in ([González et al. 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/gonzalez17a)), and an explicit exploration share. Letting users set the explore/exploit balance improved engagement in a melody tool ([Koyama group IUI 2021](https://koyama.xyz/project/interactive-exploration-exploitation-balancing-for-generative-melody-composition)). In Zero Engine, the existing softmax temperature (0.35) is already that knob.

## The build plan for Generate

The stages run in priority order. The quick wins come first because each makes the later ML work better: the log is its training data, the hardened score is its label, and the archive is its sampler. Every stage is verified the same way. Run `ZeroAnalyze test` (no regressions) and `gencheck` (time per click and fit count). Extend `genab` from its fixed "plain variation vs new pick" pairs to **any two Generate configurations** (feature on vs off, same seeds), then send the owner a blind pack. A stage ships only if the owner prefers it in clearly more than half the pairs. Rough scale for cost: S is under a day, M is a few days, L is a week or more.

Data rules hold throughout. Training uses engine renders, the 72 profiles, favourites, the owner's recordings and the keep/skip log. Commercial packs may appear only in stage 9's measurements. Stages 1 to 6 together are about two weeks of work and touch no DSP. Per the owner's keep-it-simple brief, they add no new controls. The only candidate UI addition, a Familiar-to-Surprise control mapped to softmax temperature and the exploration share, should wait until `genab` shows the default balance is wrong.

## Conclusion

The research shifts where the effort belongs. What makes generated patches good is the judge, not the generator, and judges fail by being gamed, not by being too simple. Zero Engine's render-and-check loop is already the architecture PhenoType, Genopatch and CTAG converged on, so the next gains come from a judge that cannot be fooled by hiss or phase tricks, from memory that keeps clicks diverse across sessions, and from silently recording what the owner keeps. The neural judge remains the right ML step, but it should come after the log and the hardened score, because those are its labels. Trained before them, it would learn today's blind spots. A proposer network is the step most likely to be over-engineering, and should be built only if the measurements show the archive and judge have run out of road.

One uncertainty stays open. Every taste-learning result cited came from 6 to 12 users on EQ, images or melodies, and no source tests these methods on one producer's synth presets. The owner's blind `genab` choices are therefore the experiment, not just the checkpoint.
