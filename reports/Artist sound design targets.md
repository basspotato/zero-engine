# Clean subs, dirty mids, tempo-synced motion win

Today's signature electronic sounds come from a few well-documented moves, not secret patches. They stack a few detuned saws or FM/wavetable sources, keep a clean mono sine sub under a distorted, compressed mid band, sync the movement (pump, wobble, gate) to the host tempo, and finish loud and dense. The artists themselves almost never publish settings. The closest things to artist-level numbers are Attack Magazine's reconstruction of Chase & Status's "Baddadan" foghorn bass (saws at 0, +7 and +19 semitones plus enveloped FM), the reported origin of Fred again..'s "Jungle" bass (a recorded faulty cable, shaped mainly by volume pumping), Mau P's sine-wave lead on "Drugs From Amsterdam", Charlotte de Witte's 303 acid with long, sparing cutoff sweeps, and Timecop1983's Juno slider settings. Every millisecond, LUFS and width number in this report is a tutorial value or an estimate unless a citation is attached. No public source gives measured loudness, centroid or stereo figures for genre one-shots, so those targets have to be checked against references with `ZeroAnalyze`. For Zero Engine, the research backs two existing decisions. The clean sub / dirty mids split (crossover about 100 Hz) matches what every source describes. High-passing leads, chords and pads is also standard practice. Fixed 150/200 Hz cutoffs, though, would clip the fundamentals of several sounds that sit low: techno and hoover leads, trap brass chords, and dub-techno chords high-passed at about 100 Hz. Five types also deliberately break the clean-sub rule: the trap 808, the techno rumble, the acid bass, the slightly stereo tech-house bass and the Fred again.. style reese.

**How to read the numbers.** A number followed by a citation is sourced: it comes from that tutorial, reconstruction or spec sheet, and almost never from the artist. A number marked *(estimate)*, and every number in the final table not marked **[s]**, is engineering judgement from the research notes, meant as a starting point for the tuner and not as fact. MIDI numbers use **C4 = 60** throughout (C1 = 24, C2 = 36, C3 = 48, C5 = 72, C6 = 84). The synthwave notes label their ranges "C3 = 60", but the numbers themselves are C4 = 60 values. The EDM notes do the same: their bass range, "MIDI 28-43, 41-98 Hz", is E1-G2 in C4 = 60 terms. This report uses the MIDI numbers and frequencies, which are internally consistent.

## Named artists left few patch numbers, but they show three building blocks

The artists the owner named are documented very unevenly. **Chase & Status** are the best covered. Attack Magazine rebuilds the "Baddadan" (2023) foghorn bass as OSC1 saw (volume 32), OSC2 saw at **+7 st** (volume 32) and OSC3 at **+19 st** (octave plus fifth) at full volume, with an FM oscillator raised by an envelope (FM amount 10) in retrigger mode at 174 BPM. The result is a "greasy, mid-rangey Reese" over a **constant separate sub**, finished with EQ, Drum Bus, saturation and limiting ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/the-baddadan-synth-bass-sound/)). MusicTech's version of the same sound uses a harmonically rich PWM wavetable with **+3 unison voices**, a sine **3 octaves up FM'ing it at 50%**, and a sub under a **Low-24 filter at about 70 Hz** ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/guides/essential-guide/chase-and-status-bass-garage-production-tutorial/)). MusicTech also says this foghorn bass has become a staple of current DnB.

**Fred again..**'s signature is a process, not a patch. The "Jungle" bass began as an iPhone recording of a dodgy cable, chopped and repitched in Logic ([MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/news/fred-again-jungle-bass-sound-dodgy-audio-cable)), and its defining feature is volume modulation that moves it out of the kick's way ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/tutorials/tips/how-to-recreate-fred-again-s-jungle-bass-sound)). His tricks include a tremolo with the stereo panning removed and its timing offset, used as a pump instead of a compressor ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/the-best-feeling-is-when-you-play-back-the-thing-youve-made-that-day-and-you-actually-feel-good-about-it-thats-the-drug-im-chasing-every-day-unlocking-the-production-tricks-that-fred-again-swears-by)). In his style, bass, risers and noise loops are all sidechained to the kick ([MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/news/make-a-fred-again-style-track-free-samples)). Attack's "Fred again-inspired" reese uses **64 unison voices, spread 10%, two saws detuned only ±0.070 semitone**, width 200%, and a slow chorus ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/fred-again-inspired-reese-basslines-with-twin-3/)). Reviewers called his Skrillex collaboration "Rumble" a "lean, mean and minimal" sub riff ([The Fader](https://thefader.com/2023/01/04/skrillex-rumble-feat-fred-again-flowdan)). For an engine, Fred again.. maps onto the Motion macro (a deep volume pump) and a lo-fi texture layer, not onto an oscillator recipe.

The other named artists are thin. For **Andy C**, the search found only career profiles, which describe a "clear sub-bass foundation" ([dropthebass.co](https://origin.dropthebass.co/artists/andy-c)). **Marshmello** appears only on Loopmasters' list of future-bass canon artists, next to Illenium, Flume, Odesza, Garrix and San Holo ([Loopmasters](https://www.loopmasters.com/genres/142-Future-Bass/products/12856-Ultimate-Future-Bass-for-Serum-Vol-3)). **Dom Dolla** appears only as an inspiration credit on a free tech-house pack of stab, chord and pad loops ([rekkerd.org](https://rekkerd.org/?p=267535)). **Whethan** returned no sources at all. Any "Marshmello pluck" or "Dom Dolla pad" profile is therefore a genre archetype, and it should be labelled that way.

Better-documented leading artists fill the gaps. **Mau P** built the drop of the Beatport #1 "Drugs From Amsterdam" around a "hypnotic sine wave", layered against a contrasting synth ([Beatportal](https://www.beatportal.com/news/mau-p-delivers-a-beatport-number-1-with-drugs-from-amsterdam)). **Charlotte de Witte**'s sound is 909 plus 303, with cutoff sweeps used sparingly and stretched over long passages, mostly at 135+ BPM ([gearnews](https://www.gearnews.com/techno-legends-gear-charlotte-de-witte/)). **I Hate Models** played all the melodic parts of an album opener (pads, bass, arps) on one Prophet-6, and ran it through distortion pedals for pads ([XLR8R](https://xlr8r.com/features/studio-essentials-i-hate-models/)). **Virtual Riot** records long hardware jams, chops them and turns them into Serum wavetables ([Antares](https://www.antarestech.com/blog/virtual-riot-interview)). That is resampling, which a real-time engine can reproduce as offline-rendered wavetables, in line with the project's ground rules. **Timecop1983** has the most concrete patch data in the whole corpus (below). **Flume**'s Sylenth1 lead is a detuned saw plus pulse through 24 dB low-pass filters, with an LFO and a mod envelope on the cutoff ([PML](https://www.productionmusiclive.com/blogs/news/flume-synth-with-sylenth1-designing-the-sound-from-lorde-tennis-court-flume-remix)). **Odesza**'s "Line of Sight" chords are saw/square pumped by a **1/8 volume LFO** ([Splice](https://splice.com/blog/hits-decoded-odesza-line-of-sight/)).

Across all of this, three building blocks recur: a stacked or FM source in the mids, a clean sub beneath it, and tempo-synced amplitude or filter movement. Era matters too. The classic Skrillex FM screech and plain LFO wobble (2010–13), the hoover (about 1991) and the Renegade reese (1994) are **older classics**. What is current in 2023–26 is the foghorn bass, resampled riddim growls, sine-led tech house and distorted hard techno.

## The clean-sub split matches the sources; fixed high-passes do not fit every type

**Where the research agrees with the engine.** Every source on bass design describes a clean sine (or low-passed triangle) sub under a separately processed mid layer. DnB producers low-pass the sub at about **60–90 Hz** and high-pass the synth at about **100–120 Hz** ([dnbforum](https://dnbforum.com/threads/do-you-just-use-sub-bass-mid-range-synth.145928/)). Output puts the clean low end below **100 Hz** and the mid bass at **200–800 Hz** ([Output](https://output.com/blog/best-plugins-for-drum-and-bass)). Dubspot's Datsik analysis keeps the sub mono and widens only above **about 200 Hz** ([Dubspot](https://blog.dubspot.com/electronic-music-theory-analyzing-dubstep-basslines-datsiks-3-fist-style)). Sustance applies distortion and widening "only from the mids up" ([Splice](https://splice.com/blog/?p=37509)). The UKG reese sub is a triangle with one voice, width 0 and only slight filter drive ([The Producer School](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/master-uk-garage-reese-bass-synthesis-in-serum-2-complete-sound-design-tutorial)). Vinyl-era elliptic EQ practice keeps "approx 100hz and below mono" ([KVR](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=8971452)). Zero Engine's Sub Split (commit b6b1794) sits in the middle of this range. One gap: no source gives a **sub-to-mid level ratio**, so the Weight macro's balance point has to come from measurement. A 30 Hz high-pass on the whole bass for subsonic hygiene is standard for tech house ([The Producer School](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/how-to-mix-sub-bass-for-tech-house-without-it-disappearing-on-club-systems)).

**Where the research disagrees.** Five sounds put character into the sub band on purpose. The **trap 808** is a sine with a fast pitch drop that is *saturated as a whole* so that it carries on small speakers ([LANDR](https://blog.landr.com/what-is-an-808/); [Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/808-bass-with-saturation/)). A split 808 can only imitate this by distorting a copy of the sine and high-passing it, and the result loses the intermodulation that a single saturated waveform has. The **techno rumble** is a kick sent to a **3–5 s, 100% wet reverb**, distorted hard and band-limited to about **30–150/200 Hz** ([The Producer School](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/how-to-create-techno-rumble-kicks-for-2025)). It is dirty *inside* the sub band by design. The **303 acid** bass sweeps a resonant peak straight through the crossover. The **tech-house bass** in one Vital build runs **2 voices at about 5% unison** "for a slightly stereo bass" ([SoundBridge](https://www.soundbridge.io/create-a-tech-house-bassline-using-soundbridge-and-vital)), while The Producer School insists on mono mode ([The Producer School](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/building-a-rolling-bassline-like-chris-stussy-the-3-note-pattern-that-defines-modern-tech-house)). The Fred again.. style reese is wide (width 200%). My recommendation is to keep the split as the default, allow per-profile exceptions for 808 and rumble (a "whole-signal drive" switch), and keep everything below about 100 Hz mono in all cases.

**High-passing melodic parts is also supported.** Kavinsky's "Nightcall" lead gets an "aggressive" low-end cut ([Syntorial](https://www.syntorial.com/preset-recipe/kavinsky-nightcall-lead/)). Juno pads raise the HPF to lose bass weight ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/?p=133239)). Melodic-dubstep chords are high-passed and then distorted ([Bradford College](https://digitalspace.bradfordcollege.ac.uk/10683756/?p=478)). Syntorial's trance supersaw uses a **12 dB/oct** high-pass ([Syntorial](https://www.syntorial.com/tag/trance-lead/)). Two findings argue against fixed 150/200 Hz values. First, Attack's dub-techno chord recipe high-passes at **about 100 Hz** ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/dub-techno-synth-chords/)). Second, several registers start below the cutoffs. C3 is 131 Hz and G3 is 196 Hz, so a 200 Hz high-pass on a techno lead played at C3–G3, on a hoover (often an octave or two lower), or a 150 Hz high-pass on trap brass chords voiced from C3 removes their fundamentals. An *(estimate)* fix: make the high-pass track the lowest note played, for example about 0.7× the note's fundamental, clamped between 100 Hz and the current 150/200 Hz caps, with a 12 dB/oct slope for chords and pads.

## Priority gaps, type by type

### Dubstep Chord: a high-passed supersaw wall, distorted after the filter

This is the melodic-dubstep drop chord of **Seven Lions, Illenium, Slander and Said The Sky**, defined as 135–150 BPM half-time with "massive detuned supersaw stacks" against FM growls ([Futureproof](https://genreexplorer.futureproofmusicschool.com/genre/melodic-dubstep)). **Source:** two oscillators, saw + saw or saw + square, at **7 unison voices** in a documented student project ([Bradford College](https://digitalspace.bradfordcollege.ac.uk/10683756/?p=478)). Forum practice is 7–8 voices with random phase and the second oscillator a few cents higher ([KVR](https://kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=7432823)), or two saws offset about **±0.1 semitone (about ±10 cents)** ([Audiotool](https://www.audiotool.com/track/dubstep_chords-z0kbtkFBh)). A bigger variant uses 16 voices per oscillator, the two panned opposite, with detune lowered until "wide but not phasey" ([ADSR](https://www.adsrsounds.com/serum-tutorials/serum-supersaw-wavetables-pro-tip/)). Illenium's pad is a "rapidly modulated wall of detuned saws and white noise" ([Syntorial](https://www.syntorial.com/preset-recipe/illenium-crawl-outta-love-pad/)), which supports adding a **5–10% noise layer** *(estimate)*. **Filter and envelope:** sources give the order (high-pass, then distortion, then an upper-mid boost) but not the cutoff. Engine values *(estimate)*: HP 150–250 Hz, which agrees with the current 150 Hz. Two envelope variants *(estimate)*: a sustained drop chord at A 5–20 ms / S 100% / R 200–500 ms, and a stab at A 0–5 / D 150–400 / S 0–30% / R 100–300 ms. A future-bass-style pitch blip at note start is documented for the related genre ([Loopmasters](https://www.loopmasters.com/articles/4372-How-To-Make-A-Future-Bass-Drop-A-Future-Bass-Production-Tutorial)) *(estimate: +2 to +12 st decaying in 30–80 ms)*. **Modulation** *(estimate)*: a 1/4 sidechain-style pump (400–429 ms at 150/140 BPM) plus a slow 0.1–0.5 Hz drift on detune or cutoff. **Distortion** *(estimate)*: moderate soft clip/tube, OTT 20–40%. **Stereo** *(estimate)*: mono below 150 Hz, side share 30–50%, correlation 0.2–0.6. **Brightness and loudness** *(estimate)*: bright, 12–20% of energy above 4 kHz, about −7 to −10 LUFS short-term. **Register** *(estimate)*: triads and 7ths over C3–C5 (48–72). Skip the optional C2 root doubling some producers use. The bass profile owns that octave, and a 150 Hz high-pass would remove it anyway.

### Synthwave Pad, Lead and FX: the Juno chorus does the widening

**Pad.** The best anchors are vintage spec sheets. The Juno-60 chorus runs at **0.4 Hz (I) and 0.6 Hz (II)** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Juno-60)) and gets its stereo from modulating left and right **180° out of phase** ([ModularGrid](https://modulargrid.net/e/modules/view/43993)). Clones disagree on the rates (0.5/0.83 Hz, or "1 Hz, 8% depth" for I+II), so 0.4–0.8 Hz is the defensible band. Roland's Jupiter-8 pad uses **two equal saws with VCO-2 fine-tuned ±7**, attack about 400 and release about 500–550 in model units, with the VCF lowered to 600–700 ([Roland](https://articles.roland.com/sound-design-jupiter-8-model-expansion-poly-presets/)). A Juno pad is saw at full plus square at half at 8', with the HPF raised ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/?p=133239)). Timecop1983's pads rely on long decay and release so notes blend ([Reverb Machine](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/timecop1983-synth-sounds/)). Juno-106 envelopes span **1.5 ms–3 s attack and 1.5 ms–12 s decay/release** ([Roland](https://support.roland.com/hc/en-us/articles/201966419-Juno-106-Technical-Specifications)). Engine values *(estimate)*: A 400–1500 ms, S 80–100%, R 1.5–4 s; LP 1–2.5 kHz with resonance at most 15%; PWM LFO 0.3–1 Hz; width from chorus, not unison (side share 35–50%); centroid about 1–2 kHz; −16 to −18 LUFS short-term; register 48–72. The current 150 Hz high-pass fits. Exemplars: **The Midnight**, who call the Juno-6 their "beating heart" ([inkl/MusicRadar](https://www.inkl.com/news/if-the-porsche-911-is-the-platonic-ideal-of-a-sports-car-and-the-submariner-rolex-is-the-ideal-of-a-watch-the-juno-6-is-that-for-the-midnight-it-s-in-its-beating-heart-tyler-lyle-talks-essential-gear-sax-appeal-and-taking-synthwave-on-the-road)), and Timecop1983.

**Lead.** Timecop1983's "Lovers" lead mixes a pulse into the saw, with PWM in LFO mode and Chorus I. On TAL U-NO-LX sliders (0–10), "Eyes Closed" runs **cutoff 3, resonance 2, env 5, ADSR 2/5/6/7, both choruses** ([Reverb Machine](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/timecop1983-synth-sounds/)). "On The Run" adds a detuned saw, LFOs on phase and fine-tune, a flanger, Erosion, and sidechain to the kick ([Synth Ctrl](https://synthctrl.com/blogs/blog/timecop1983-on-the-run-breakdown)). The "Nightcall" lead is a **3-voice unison saw** with a resonant low-pass decay and 80s chorus ([Syntorial](https://www.syntorial.com/preset-recipe/kavinsky-nightcall-lead/)). The CS-80-style brass lead is mono, **attack 250 ms, decay 1900 ms**, through a 12 dB filter at low resonance ([Syntorial](https://www.syntorial.com/preset-recipe/vangelis-blade-runner-brass/); [Moog forum](https://forum.moogmusic.com/viewtopic.php?p=112587)). Engine values *(estimate)*: saw + PWM pulse, 2–3 voices at 8–15 cents; A 5–20 / D 300–800 / S 50–70% / R 300–600 ms; cutoff 1.5–3 kHz, resonance 15–25%; delayed vibrato at 4.5–6 Hz, ±10–25 cents, starting after 200–500 ms; dotted-1/8 delay at 25–40% feedback; side share 20–35%; −12 to −14 LUFS. The darksynth variant (**Perturbator**, who put distortion across his synths from *New Model* on, per [mxdwn](https://music.mxdwn.com/2017/11/05/reviews/perturbator-new-model/)) adds 6–15 dB drive, a 30–80 ms glide and sits about 2 dB louder. Register 60–84. The 200 Hz high-pass agrees with Nightcall's low cut.

**FX.** This is the least-sourced cell in the whole matrix. Only the generic riser (white noise through a low-pass that opens over time) is documented ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/synth-sweep-fx)), along with the gated-reverb snare: a **2–3 s reverb** gated from the dry hit with fast attack and release ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/tutorials/tips/how-to-create-authentic-vintage-drum-gated-reverb-for-synthwave-chillwave-music-styles)). Attack's synthwave "laser-gun" bass uses a filter envelope with zero attack, high decay and sustain, and raised resonance ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/how-to-make-a-synthwave-laser-gun-bassline/)). A synthwave FX profile *(estimate)* should cycle through a 4-bar noise sweep (LP 200 Hz to 10 kHz plus chorus and a 3 s hall), a laser zap (saw/square pitch dropping 2–3 octaves in 150–300 ms into a dotted-1/8 delay), a reverse pad swell, and a gated-reverb impact cut at about 250–400 ms. Fully wide, −14 to −18 LUFS. No artist interviews about FX design were found for Perturbator, Carpenter Brut, Lazerhawk or FM-84.

### Tech House Bass and Pad: a mono sine that pumps, and a chord loop rather than a wash

**Bass** is the best-sourced gap. The Fisher / Chris Lake / Cloonee / Michael Bibi model is a **simple sine or square, light saturation, sidechained to the kick** ([Beatportal](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/692607-step-by-step-guide-to-producing-tech-house-like-fisher-chris-lake-cloonee-and-michael-bibi)). Chris Stussy's rolling bass is a **sine in mono mode** on a 3-note pattern, with one note changed per bar ([The Producer School](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/building-a-rolling-bassline-like-chris-stussy-the-3-note-pattern-that-defines-modern-tech-house)). A Vital build uses square + saw with a 24 dB slope, **resonance 0, cutoff about 150 Hz**, and a small plucky envelope on the cutoff ([SoundBridge](https://soundbridge.io/en/create-a-tech-house-bassline-using-soundbridge-and-vital)). Other guides low-pass everything above **200–300 Hz** ([SampleFocus](https://samplefocus.com/blog/make-tech-house-track-chris-lake/)) and map velocity to cutoff on offbeat notes ([The Producer School](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/master-modern-tech-house-bass-lines-complete-tutorial-guide)). Engine values *(estimate)*: sine plus 0–30% square/saw in the mid layer only; filter envelope 0 / 80–180 ms / 0–20% / 50 ms at +1 to +2 octaves; amp 1–3 / 150–250 / 60–80% / 30–60 ms; 1/4 pump at 124–128 BPM (476 ms at 126); Grit 10–25% soft clip above about 120 Hz; side share under 5%; under 2% energy above 4 kHz; −8 to −10 LUFS short-term with PLR 8–12 dB; register E1–G2 (28–43). This is Zero Engine's clean-sub template almost exactly. The only open question is whether to allow the 5% unison width above the crossover. A **donk** variant (sine/triangle with a +12 to +24 st pitch blip and a band-pass knock, C2–C3) is an established category, but no numeric recipe was found ([PML](https://www.productionmusiclive.com/blogs/news/5-basslines-every-producer-should-know-max-styler-serum-2)).

**Pad** has no genre-specific tutorial at all. Packs treat the tech-house "pad" as filtered chord-stab loops and vocal pads ([rekkerd.org](https://rekkerd.org/?p=267535)), with chord loops labelled at 125 BPM ([Loopmasters](https://www.loopmasters.com/products/8980)). The nearest sourced recipe is Attack's dub-techno chord: **−7 dB at 600 Hz with a wide Q, HP about 100 Hz, LP about 4.5 kHz**, low-wet plate and tape delay ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/dub-techno-synth-chords/)). I recommend building the Tech House Pad as a **filtered, pumping minor-7th/9th chord pad** rather than an ambient wash *(estimate)*: 2 saws at 5–10 cents, A 300–800 / S 100% / R 1.5–3 s, LP 12 dB at 1–3 kHz with a 1–4-bar cutoff LFO at ±10–20%, a 1/4 pump ducking 6–10 dB, side share 30–45%, 5–12% above 4 kHz, −13 to −16 LUFS, register 48–72. The editorial exemplars, Dom Dolla and John Summit, rest on genre association, not documentation.

### Trap Chord, Pad and Pluck: festival brass versus rap-trap keys

Trap divides into two palettes, and each of these profiles has to pick one. **Chord, festival flavour:** RL Grime's "trademark brass horn synths" ([EDMTunes](https://www.edmtunes.com/?p=86801)), and a trap course that lists "distorted horn stabs" and "modulated super saw chords" as staples ([Groove3/Muse](https://www-cf.musehub.com/course/g3-course-trap-sound-design-explained)). Engine values *(estimate)*: a saw stack whose low-pass envelope opens over 30–80 ms (the "brass" swell), A 5–30 / S 100% / R 200–400 ms, distorted, OTT 30–60%, side share 30–50%, −9 to −11 LUFS. **Chord, rap flavour:** Metro Boomin's "polished but slightly ominous" layered keys, vintage polysynths and choirs (a listening analysis, not documentation; [Internet Tattoo](https://www.internettattoo.com/blog/what-vst-plugins-does-metro-boomin-use)). That means low unison, a low-pass filter, OTT at 0–25%, and about −12 to −14 LUFS *(estimate)*. Register C3–C5 (48–72) puts brass fundamentals at 131 Hz, *below* the engine's 150 Hz chord high-pass, so this profile needs the register-tracking high-pass or a 100–120 Hz cap.

**Pad** *(estimate)*: a dark choir/string pad with low unison, heavy reverb and a static LP at 1–4 kHz; A 200–800 ms, R 1–3 s; low energy above 4 kHz; −12 to −16 LUFS; register C3–G4 (48–67). Pierre Bourne's reported toolset (Omnisphere 2, FM8, Absynth, Twin 2, u-he ACE; [Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/pierre-bourne)) points to preset-library pads rather than designed patches.

**Pluck:** the generic recipe is sourced (near-instant attack, short-to-medium decay, zero sustain, short release, filter starting open and closing during the decay; [Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-make-a-pluck-sound/)). The trap-specific colour is the **bell**, as in Metro's bell-like plucks and Pierre Bourne's FM8. Engine values *(estimate)*: 2-operator FM (1:1 for an e-piano tone, about 1:3.5 or another non-integer ratio for a bell) or a soft saw/square; A 0–2 / D 200–600 / S 0 / R 150–400 ms with an FM-index or LP decay; PLR 12–16 dB; −11 to −14 LUFS; register C4–C6, with bells up to C7 (60–96).

### Techno Lead and Bass: hoover, distorted poly, or acid and rumble

**Lead.** Peak-time and hard techno lean on two sources. The first is the **hoover**: the Alpha Juno "What the?" patch from about 1991, three oscillators an octave apart with heavy PWM and thick chorus ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoover_sound)). It is a classic sound, not current mainstream, though KlangKuenstler- and Landry-style rave leads borrow from it. The second is the **distorted poly**: I Hate Models' Prophet-6 through pedals ([XLR8R](https://xlr8r.com/features/studio-essentials-i-hate-models/)), and Sara Landry's "high-definition", industrial hard techno ([Ableton](https://www.ableton.com/fr/blog/sara-landry-high-end-hard-techno/)). Melodic techno leads (Afterlife, Anyma; no patch source) use short attack, fast decay and a filter envelope ([MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/how-to/how-to-program-a-melodic-techno-synth-sound)). Hard-lead values *(estimate)*: detuned saws or a PWM octave stack, LP 12 dB at 3–8 kHz, A 5 ms / S 100% / R 150 ms, Grit 50–80% with a post-LP at 8–10 kHz to remove fizz, 1/16 step gate or dotted-1/8 delay, side share 10–25% (club leads stay central), 15–30% above 4 kHz, PLR 6–9 dB, −9 to −11 LUFS. Melodic variant *(estimate)*: LP 24 dB at 600–2000 Hz, resonance 20–40%, A 2 / D 300 / S 50% / R 200 ms, 8–15% above 4 kHz, side 20–35%. Tempo runs 120–128 for melodic, 135+ for peak-time ([The Producer School](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/how-to-create-techno-music-like-charlotte-de-witte)) and 140–155 for hard. Register C3–C5 (48–72), with hoovers lower. This conflicts with the engine's 200 Hz lead high-pass.

**Bass.** There are two current flavours, and neither is a clean sine. **Acid** (de Witte, Amelie Lens by association) is a saw or square through a resonant low-pass, cutoff starting around "10 o'clock", resonance high, glide on slides, and overdrive *after* the filter ([SoundBridge](https://www.soundbridge.io/design-your-own-303-sound-from-scratch); [Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/acid-synth-uhe-diva/)). Engine values *(estimate)*: LP 24 dB at 200–600 Hz, resonance 70–90%, filter envelope 0 / 150–300 ms / 0 at +2 to +4 octaves, glide 60–100 ms, Motion as a 4–8-bar cutoff sweep to match de Witte's long sweeps, Grit 30–60%, 5–12% above 4 kHz, register C2–C4 (36–60). **Rumble** (hard techno) is the distorted kick-reverb tail described above, with an Auto Filter around **96.6 Hz** ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/?p=63028)) or a LP from about **250 Hz** ([Bonedo](https://www.bonedo.de/artikel/bass-drum-rumble-rattle-for-techno)), ducked on the kick. Engine values *(estimate)*: noise/sine tail low-passed at 90–250 Hz, drive 60–90%, decaying into the next kick (300–450 ms at 135–145 BPM), 1/4 duck recovering in 150–250 ms, register A0–F1 (21–29), fully mono. A rolling square arpeggio with cutoff modulation is a third, gentler option ([SoundBridge](https://www.soundbridge.io/deep-analog-techno-bass-tutorial)). Since an engine one-shot cannot get a kick input, the rumble has to be synthesised as a tail and ducked by a tempo-synced shaper.

### EDM Bass: the foghorn stack over a constant sub

EDM Bass covers four sub-styles. Choosing one default matters more than covering them all. The sourced candidates are the **foghorn** stack (0/+7/+19 st saws, enveloped FM, retrigger, saturation and limiting) and the **UKG/house** pair: a mono triangle/sine sub an octave under an organ, square or reese mid ([The Producer School](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/master-uk-garage-reese-bass-synthesis-in-serum-2-complete-sound-design-tutorial); [Loopmasters](https://www.loopmasters.com/products/12026)). The **future-bass** bass (Illenium/Marshmello: sine sub plus a saw/reese mid following the chord root) and the **big-room** offbeat bass (Garrix/SHM) are estimates only. I recommend the foghorn as the default *(estimate)*: it is the most current crossover sound, it already fits the Sub Split, and its +7/+19 partials give strong energy at 1.5× and 3× the root, which reads on small speakers. Engine values: sine sub below about 100 Hz; mid of 3 saws at 0/+7/+19 st with the top one loudest and 1–3 unison voices each; FM amount rising on a 0–10 ms attack / 80–250 ms decay envelope *(estimate)*; amp sustain 100% with a release of about **24 ms**, as in Attack's reese ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/tutorials/reese-bass-redux/)); mid LP 1–3 kHz; Motion as a Fred again.. style 1/4 volume pump (near-full duck on bass) *(estimate)*; Grit as drive into OTT at 50–100% on the mid only; correlation above +0.5, side share under 10%; register E1–G2 (28–43); −6 to −9 LUFS short-term with PLR 6–9 dB *(estimate)*. Club DnB drops master around −4 to −6 LUFS ([Mastering The Mix](https://www.masteringthemix.com/blogs/learn/mixing-for-streaming-platforms-vs-clubs-key-differences)), and festival EDM around −6 to −7 ([Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-master-edm/)), so a bass one-shot that feeds such a drop has to be dense.

## Loudness and brightness targets are estimates; tempo-synced motion is well sourced

The weakest link is the finished-one-shot measurements. Searches found no published LUFS, centroid, side-share or PLR for any genre's one-shots. Sample packs list formats, not loudness ([Loopmasters](https://www.loopmasters.com/genres/49-Dubstep/products/18178-Phuture-Sounds?bid=13923)). The only sourced loudness anchors are **mix-level**: club/festival masters at −6 to −9 LUFS integrated, big room and festival trap at −6 to −7, deep house and techno at −8 to −10, true peak at −1 dBTP ([Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-master-edm/)); one techno producer's masters at about −8 LUFS / −0.7 dBTP ([SoundBetter](https://soundbetter.com/profiles/684331-arkeØn)); and general masters at −10 to −14 ([Waves](https://www.waves.com/how-many-lufs-should-my-master-be)). The per-type LUFS and brightness values in the table below are therefore judgement. They are useful as tuner seeds, but `ZeroAnalyze compare/tune` against the owner's references should override them as soon as a group has three or more references.

Motion, by contrast, is well sourced and easy to implement. Wobbles run at **1/4, 1/8, 1/8T and 1/16**, with 1/16–1/32 for the fast "3 Fist" style ([Dubspot](https://blog.dubspot.com/electronic-music-theory-analyzing-dubstep-basslines-datsiks-3-fist-style); [MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/how-to/lfo-wobble-bass)). Modern growls modulate wavetable position and warp instead of only cutoff ([Bass Gorilla](https://bassgorilla.com/serum-tutorial-super-growl-bass-in-ableton-live/)). Odesza's chords pump at 1/8. Fred again.., tech house and rumble all duck at 1/4. One calculation from the notes is worth keeping: at 140 BPM, 1/8T is 7.0 Hz and 1/16 is 9.33 Hz; at 174 BPM, 1/8 is 5.8 Hz. The same division therefore lands at very different Hz across genres, so the engine should sync Motion to host tempo rather than use fixed rates. OTT is documented as a dry/wet with "5–20% subtle, 20–60% by instrument, 100% creative" ([Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/items/xfer-records-ott-multiband-compressor-plugin)). The Noisia-style chain is overdrive at 25% into OTT at 100%, then a +10 dB saturator into another OTT ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/learn-how-to-make-noisia-inspired-synths-with-abletons-operator/)). Tech house and techno sources barely mention OTT, which suggests (as an estimate) low depth there to keep transient punch.

## Conclusion

The research mostly confirms Zero Engine's design. The biggest lesson is about what *not* to expect: published "signature patches" for the named artists essentially do not exist, and the artists who explain themselves (Fred again.., Virtual Riot, Mau P) describe workflows like recording, resampling, pumping and keeping it minimal, not knob values. The engine's edge is therefore not in matching a famous preset. It is in getting four shared mechanics right: a fixed mono sub crossover near 100 Hz with per-profile exceptions for the 808 and the rumble; host-synced Motion that pumps as readily as it wobbles; a mid-band-only Grit chain of drive into OTT; and a high-pass that tracks register so low leads and brass chords keep their fundamentals.

The next gain will come from measurement, not more reading. The table's loudness, brightness and width columns are the least sourced and the most important to the tuner. Each gap profile needs three or more owner-supplied or CC0 references under `<Genre>/<Type>/`, so that `ZeroAnalyze tune` can replace these estimates with measured means. The most urgent are Synthwave FX, Tech House Pad and the Trap pads and plucks, where the literature has nothing concrete to offer.
